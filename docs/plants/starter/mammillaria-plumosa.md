# Feather cactus

- Inventory: Starter-05 — _Mammillaria plumosa_
- Label ID: `A2`
- Tracker ID: `P02`
- Visual description: Low clustering mound hidden beneath soft-looking white featherlike spines.
- Interesting fact: Each “feather” is actually a comb of many tiny spines, so the fluffy surface is still defensive.

## Names and identity

| Kind                    | Name                              |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Accepted botanical name | _Mammillaria plumosa_ F.A.C.Weber |
| Common names            | Feather cactus, feather ball      |
| Name clue               | _plumosa_ means feathered         |

What looks like cotton is the cactus's normal armament. Each areole produces a
ring of finely divided white radial spines that resemble tiny feathers and
largely hide the green body. Unlike loose cottony mealybug wax, the natural
feathers repeat in an orderly pattern from every tubercle.

## Origin, form, and flowers

The species is native to northeastern Mexico, principally Coahuila and Nuevo
León, where it grows on limestone-rich slopes and cliffs in dry shrubland.
Individual heads are small and rounded, but they offset freely until an old
plant can form a broad white cushion.

Creamy-white to pale yellow or dull pink flowers appear in a ring near the top
of the heads, often from late summer into the cooler season in cultivation.
They can be partly concealed by the spines. The fruit develops within the wool
and is much less conspicuous than the bright berries of many other
_Mammillaria_ species.

Frédéric Albert Constantin Weber published the species in 1898. Kew accepts the
name and reports an IUCN status of Near Threatened. Its specialized limestone
habitat and popularity with collectors make documented nursery propagation
preferable to plants without provenance.

## Care in this collection

| Topic               | Practical approach                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Grow-light position | Bright but not an abrupt close-center blast. Start around 10–17 mol/m²/day DLI, approximately 230–395 µmol/m²/s over 12 hours.                   |
| Water               | Wet the mix thoroughly and let it drain; then wait for a full dry-down. Keep water out of a cool crown and reduce strongly in winter.            |
| Pot and mix         | A wide, shallow pot can suit an expanding clump. The current individual pot is fine until heads reach its rim. Favor excellent mineral drainage. |
| Temperature         | A dry plant handles cool nights better than a damp one. Protect from hard frost and cold rain.                                                   |
| Feeding             | Dilute cactus feed during active growth only; soft fast growth is not an improvement.                                                            |

Water the soil, not the wool. If the feathers get wet, airflow and warmth should
let them dry quickly. Persistent mineral spotting is cosmetic; persistent damp
at the hidden body can become structural.

## Propagation and watch points

- Offsets can be separated once they have enough size to root; seed is also
  straightforward with warmth and controlled moisture.
- Inspect with a bright light and a wooden skewer rather than parting the wool
  repeatedly. Mechanical handling can mat or break the feathered spines.
- Mealybugs are the main camouflage problem. New irregular cotton, sticky
  residue, or insects around the roots deserve attention.
- The green body can be hard to see, so use firmness and the appearance of the
  growing center. A beige outer patch may be stained wool; a soft dark body
  beneath it is different.
