# Ming Thing

- Inventory: Starter-10 — _Cereus forbesii_ 'Ming Thing'
- Label ID: `D3`
- Tracker ID: `P12`
- Visual description: A dense blue-green cluster of knobby, brainlike stems with no single regular column, each lobe folding into the next to create the cultivar's compact monstrose texture.
- Interesting fact: The strange form is not always permanent in every growing point: a 'Ming Thing' can occasionally revert and send out a normal-looking _Cereus_ shoot from the monstrose mass.

| Underlying accepted species | _Cereus forbesii_ C.F.Först.               |
| Rejected nursery label      | “_Opuntia cylindrica cristata_” or similar |

The original _Opuntia_ label does not fit. _Opuntia_ relatives show jointed
segments and distinctive glochid-bearing areoles; this plant makes dense,
knobby rib fragments characteristic of 'Ming Thing'.

## Species background

Normal _Cereus forbesii_ is a branching columnar cactus native from Bolivia
through Paraguay into north-central Argentina. It grows in seasonally dry
shrubland, eventually becoming a small cactus tree. Large pale nocturnal
flowers and colored fleshy fruits look nothing like the tiny distorted growing
points of the nursery cultivar.

Charles Förster published the species in 1846. Kew accepts it and includes
_Cereus hankeanus_, another name common in older horticulture, in its synonymy.
The normal species is assessed as Least Concern.

NC State Extension describes 'Ming Thing' as a naturally occurring monstrose
mutation with multiple growing points. It rarely flowers. In the normal species,
the large night-opening flowers produce a sweet fragrance that attracts moths
and other nighttime pollinators.

## What monstrose growth means

Growth is distributed among many irregular growing points. That
creates miniature towers, fans, and lumps. Shape changes are normal from one
season to the next. Health is better judged by firmness, color, roots, and
compact new growth than by symmetry.

## Propagation and watch points

- A clean monstrose piece can be rooted after a complete callus, preserving the
  form vegetatively.
- Straight normal growth is a reversion, not etiolation if it is thick and
  well-spined. It can outgrow the monstrose portion.
- Mealybugs and scale can disappear into the maze of growth points. Photograph
  the same angle monthly so newly white or brown material stands out.
- Water left between knobs encourages localized rot. Water the mix and maintain
  airflow rather than misting the body.
- A small dead knob can dry in place without threatening the whole plant; a
  soft spreading discolored area requires prompt inspection.
