## Names and identity

| Kind                    | Name                                                                |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Accepted botanical name | _Astrophytum ornatum_ (DC.) Britton & Rose                          |
| Common names            | Monk's hood cactus, star cactus, bishop's cap cactus                |
| Original name           | _Echinocactus ornatus_ DC.                                          |
| Name clue               | _Astrophytum_ means star plant; _ornatum_ means decorated or ornate |

Viewed from above, the strong ribs produce a star. White woolly flecks decorate
the green epidermis, while long yellow-brown spines distinguish it from the
usually spineless _A. myriostigma_ that also gets called bishop's cap.

## Origin, history, and mature form

The species is endemic to Mexico, occurring from the northeast toward Veracruz
in dry valleys, canyon walls, and rocky limestone country. It is the largest
and eventually tallest _Astrophytum_: a young globe slowly develops into a
substantial column with usually six to eight ribs.

Bright yellow flowers emerge from the woolly crown on mature plants. The RHS
places flowering maturity on a long horizon—often 10–20 years under cultivation.
That is normal for a slow species rather than evidence that a small plant is
being cared for incorrectly.

Augustin de Candolle described it as _Echinocactus ornatus_ in 1828. Britton
and Rose published the accepted _Astrophytum_ combination in 1922. Kew reports
the wild species as Vulnerable, reinforcing the value of seed-grown,
nursery-documented plants.

## Care in this collection

| Topic               | Practical approach                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Grow-light position | High-light zone after acclimation: roughly 12–22 mol/m²/day DLI, or 280–510 µmol/m²/s averaged over 12 hours.                                               |
| Outdoor light       | Gradually increase direct sun. Flecking provides some shade but does not make a nursery plant burn-proof.                                                   |
| Water               | Thorough soak followed by a complete dry-down during warm growth; keep cool winter roots nearly dry.                                                        |
| Mix                 | Very free-draining and mineral. A slightly alkaline component is compatible with its limestone habitat, but drainage matters more than chasing an exact pH. |
| Pot                 | It develops a substantial root system; use enough depth without surrounding a small root ball with a huge wet volume.                                       |
| Feeding             | Low-strength cactus fertilizer during active growth only.                                                                                                   |

Compact growth keeps the gaps between areoles short and the crown well armed.
Elongation cannot be reversed, so tune the lamp based on the next band of
growth, not old proportions.

## Propagation and watch points

- Seed is the usual method. The species does not normally offer plentiful
  offsets.
- Water and debris can lodge in the woolly crown; keep it open and ventilated.
- Spider mites scar exposed green skin, while root mealybugs can cause an
  unexplained loss of vigor.
- White flecks are natural trichomes. Do not scrub them off as scale, and avoid
  frequent handling.
- Mature spines are stiff and long. Move the pot with folded paper, tongs
  protected by padding, or gloves that cannot snag.
