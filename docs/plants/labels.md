# Pot labels

Last updated: 2026-08-10

This is the print reference for the 27 living plants in the collection. Pot
labels use only the short permanent ID, such as `A1` or `B2`. These IDs identify
plants; they are not physical row/column coordinates. The longer
inventory IDs remain in the collection records so the historical documentation
and photo archive do not need to be renamed.

The six August cactus labels are `E1`–`E3` and `F1`–`F3`; their exact mapping
was recorded from the pots on 2026-08-10. The numbered mapping was also
recorded that day: `#1` is the shared rehab cactus planter, `#2` is the shared
succulent planter, `#3` is the money tree, and `#4` is the Kiwi aeonium.

The ID-to-plant mapping comes from the
[AW200SE and AeroWave operating schedule](../equipment/aw200se-operating-schedule.md#final-pot-layout).

## SUPVAN E11 print setup

The SUPVAN E11 is a 203 dpi monochrome thermal printer that accepts media up to
15 mm wide. The supplied media includes 15 mm continuous tape and 12 × 40 mm
die-cut labels. The continuous tape is the better choice for long botanical
names and origins; the die-cut labels are best reserved for short ID or
orientation markers.

Use the SUPVAN app for these labels. Its saved templates, multiline text, and
adjustable label length are more practical than entering every botanical name
from the keyboard.

For each individually potted starter plant, print two 15 mm continuous strips:

```text
B2 | OLD MAN OF THE ANDES
Oreocereus trollii
```

```text
NATIVE: BOLIVIA + NW ARGENTINA
DESC: 1929 | WARN: SPINES UNDER WOOL
```

Use two lines per strip, a plain sans-serif font, and the largest text that
fits. Italics are optional; legibility matters more on a 15 mm label. Let the
printer choose the length rather than shrinking a long scientific name into a
40 mm die-cut label.

The E11 listing describes its laminated labels as water-, oil-, heat-, and
scratch-resistant, but the same comparison material positions the E11 mainly
for indoor use. Treat the labels as water-resistant rather than permanent
outdoor tags. Clean and dry the pot before applying one, keep it off the saucer
edge, and inspect the adhesive after outdoor heat or watering.

Source: [SUPVAN E11 Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DKS89T75?th=1).

## Rotation and orientation

Do not print `ROT90` stickers for the ordinary individual pots. Put the
common-name label on the side facing the inspection aisle. That label is the
orientation marker: every Sunday after the light turns off, rotate the pot 90°
clockwise. The label will return to the aisle after four turns.

Only the variegated moon cactus and two fixed shared planters need a
nonstandard orientation label:

```text
A1 | DO NOT ROUTINELY ROTATE
KEEP PALE SIDE OUT / AWAY FROM LIGHT CENTER
```

```text
#2 | SHARED SUCCULENT PLANTER
ECHEVERIA SIDE FACES IN / LIGHT CENTER
```

```text
#1 | SHARED REHAB CACTUS PLANTER
PALE BLUE TORCH FACES DIVIDER / OUT
MONKEY TAIL TRAILS OPEN ROOM ARC
```

The A1 marker can use a 12 × 40 mm die-cut label if the text stays readable.
The two planter instructions are better on 15 mm continuous tape. The former
`A4-B4` and `C4-D4` IDs remain only as historical cross-references.

## What “described” means

`DESC` is the earliest formal botanical publication year for the species or
named taxon, not a literal discovery date. Local people may have known a plant
long before its scientific publication, and an exact collection or discovery
date is often unavailable.

For a cultivar, variegated selection, probable hybrid, or monstrose clone, the
native range and date belong to the underlying species unless the table says
otherwise. A trade selection's breeder, nursery origin, or release year is not
filled in without a reliable record.

`PROB.` means the identification is still photo-based and may change.

## Identification and origin research outcome

- Kew botanical records now support the wild ranges and publication years in
  the tables. The golden torch reference date was corrected from 1840 to 1839,
  and the copper spoons range was kept at the defensible “Madagascar” level.
- NC State Extension confirms 'Ming Thing' as the monstrose cultivar name for
  _Cereus forbesii_. No reliable breeder, nursery-origin, or release year was
  found.
- Current retail records confirm Indigo Wave as an Altman Reserve
  _Myrtillocactus_ trade selection. Sellers often call it crested or
  `f. cristata`, but no reliable breeder or release record was found.
- Current retail records confirm Dragon's Egg as an Altman Plants _Euphorbia_
  trade name. They do not publish its parentage, so the
  _E. obesa_-type-hybrid identification stays probable.
- SANBI confirms that _Portulacaria afra_ 'Aurea' is a cultivated yellow form.
  The collection plant has no cultivar label, so color alone is not enough to
  assign that name.
- The echeveria, silver teaspoons, blue torch, and golden torch still need
  flowers or stronger diagnostic photos for a species-level confirmation.

## Other information worth recording

The requested display fields plus the warning are enough for the visible
labels. Other useful information belongs in the repo or on a replaceable label
under the pot:

1. **Acquisition date and source** — add when a receipt or order history can
   establish it; do not guess.
2. **Last repotted date** — useful under the pot or in a care log.
3. **Full inventory ID** — keep it in the repo records; the short permanent ID
   is intentionally the only ID printed on the visible label.

Do not put a fixed watering interval or “next watering” date on a permanent
label. Drying time changes with season, light, plant size, and pot position.
Record actual watering and repotting events separately.

## Source notes

Native ranges and publication years follow the linked collection profiles and
their botanical references, principally Kew Plants of the World Online and
IPNI. Safety wording for _Kalanchoe_ follows the
[ASPCA plant record](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/kalanchoe);
the _Euphorbia_ warning follows the
[Royal Horticultural Society](https://www.rhs.org.uk/prevention-protection/potentially-harmful-garden-plants).

Important date conventions:

- The red-spined rainbow form was published at varietal rank in 1982 and moved
  to the accepted subspecies rank in 1997.
- _Gymnocalycium mihanovichii_ was published under _Echinocactus_ in 1905 and
  transferred to _Gymnocalycium_ in 1922.
- _Portulacaria afra_ was published under _Crassula_ in 1762; the current
  replacement name dates to 1787.
- Peter MacOwan collected _Euphorbia obesa_ in 1897, but its formal botanical
  publication dates to 1903.
