# Pot labels

Last updated: 2026-07-25

This is the label-maker reference for the 19 living plants in the collection.
It uses the inventory IDs and final table slots from the
[AW200SE and AeroWave operating schedule](../equipment/aw200se-operating-schedule.md#final-pot-layout).

## Recommended label format

Use four lines where the label size allows:

```text
STARTER-01 | B2 | ROT90
OLD MAN OF THE ANDES
Oreocereus trollii
NATIVE: BOLIVIA-NW ARGENTINA | DESC. 1929
```

The fields mean:

- **Inventory ID** is the durable connection between the pot, collection
  record, photos, and care notes.
- **Slot** restores the pot to the intended light zone if it is moved.
- **Common name** is the quick everyday name.
- **Botanical name** is the working identification. `PROB.` means the
  photo-based ID may change.
- **Native** means the wild range of the underlying species, not the nursery or
  store that produced this particular plant.
- **Desc.** is the earliest formal botanical publication year for the species
  or named form. This is more accurate than “discovered”: local people may have
  known a plant long before its scientific publication, and an exact discovery
  date is often unavailable.

For a cultivar, variegated selection, probable hybrid, or monstrose clone, the
native range and year belong to the underlying species unless the label says
otherwise. The horticultural selection's own origin may be unknown.

## Rotation codes

| Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `ROT90` | Every Sunday after the light finishes, turn the pot 90° clockwise without moving it to another slot. |
| `FIX-PALE-OUT` | Do not rotate blindly; keep Starter-07's palest tissue facing away from the center of the light. |
| `FIX` | Do not routinely rotate the shared planter; preserve its documented orientation. |

Put the printed identity label on the side currently facing the inspection
aisle. That gives the pot an obvious “front” and makes each 90° turn visible.
The label will move around the pot over four Sundays and return to the aisle
side after the fourth turn.

## Other information worth labeling

The four requested botanical fields are enough for a display label. The most
useful additions are:

1. **Inventory ID** — strongly recommended; it connects the physical plant to
   its repo record and prevents similar-looking pots from being mixed up.
2. **Slot and rotation code** — strongly recommended for the grow-light table.
3. **Acquisition date and source** — useful provenance if receipts or order
   history can establish them. Do not guess; start recording these for future
   plants.
4. **Last repotted date** — useful, but put it on a replaceable label underneath
   the pot or in a care log rather than rebuilding the display label.
5. **Safety warning** — especially `CAUSTIC SAP` for Starter-11.

Do not put a fixed watering interval or “next watering” date on a permanent
label. Drying time changes with season, light, plant size, and pot position.
Record actual watering and repotting events separately.

## Short-label fallback

If the tape is too narrow for four lines, keep the ID and scientific name on
the pot and move the other information to this document:

```text
STARTER-01 | B2 | ROT90
Oreocereus trollii
```

The inventory ID is more useful on a small label than trying to squeeze in a
long native range.

## Source notes

Native ranges and publication years follow the linked collection profiles and
their botanical references. In particular:

- Kew records the original *Echinocactus phyllacanthus* publication in 1836
  and the accepted *Stenocactus* combination in 1929.
- The red-spined rainbow form was first published at varietal rank in 1982 and
  moved to the accepted subspecies rank in 1997.
- *Gymnocalycium mihanovichii* was first published under *Echinocactus* in
  1905 and transferred to *Gymnocalycium* in 1922.
- The taxon now called *Portulacaria afra* was first published under
  *Crassula* in 1762; the current replacement name dates to 1787.
- Peter MacOwan collected *Euphorbia obesa* in 1897, but its formal botanical
  publication dates to 1903.
