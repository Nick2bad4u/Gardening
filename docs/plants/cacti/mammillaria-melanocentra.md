# Black-centered pincushion cactus

- Inventory: Cactus-03 — _Mammillaria_ cf. _melanocentra_
- Label ID: `E3`
- Identification: **probable; _Mammillaria mystax_ is the principal alternative**
- Acquired from: Carlsons' Greenhouse, Torrey Road, Fenton, Michigan; exact date not recorded
- Current pot: 4-inch individual pot
- Photo archive: [licensed species-reference images](../../../assets/plants/mammillaria-melanocentra/)

## Names and identity

| Kind                   | Name                             |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Working identification | _Mammillaria_ cf. _melanocentra_ |
| Common name            | Black-centered pincushion cactus |
| Main alternative       | _Mammillaria mystax_             |
| Permanent-label form   | PROB. _Mammillaria melanocentra_ |

The working match is a woolly globe with large tubercles and dark central
spines. The red projection described with the identification is a club-shaped
fruit rather than a flower. Keep `PROB.` on the permanent label until clearer
tubercle, axil, spine, flower, and fruit evidence separates it from
_M. mystax_.

## Origin, form, and publication

Kew accepts _Mammillaria melanocentra_, first published in 1855, and places
its native range in southeastern Coahuila and western Nuevo León, Mexico. It
grows in desert and dry shrubland.

## Care in this collection

| Topic               | Practical approach                                                                                    |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Grow-light position | Bright middle-to-outer cactus zone while the photo-only ID and light response are being observed.     |
| Water               | Soak, drain, and allow the 4-inch pot to dry completely; keep a cool woolly crown from remaining wet. |
| Pot and mix         | Keep the current pot until the body or offsets crowd the rim; do not bury the lower tubercles.        |
| Fruit               | Leave attached fruit alone until it loosens naturally unless rot or pests appear around it.           |

## Rotation, propagation, and watch points

- Rotate 90° clockwise during the normal Sunday rotation.
- Seed can be collected from mature fruit; offsets, if produced, can be rooted.
- Inspect woolly axils for mealybugs without forcing the tubercles apart.
- Save close photographs of flowers and mature fruit because they may resolve
  the identification.
