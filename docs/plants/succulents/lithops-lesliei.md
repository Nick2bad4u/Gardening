## Botanical context

Kew accepts _Lithops lesliei_ as a succulent perennial, with the current name
published in 1922 and its earlier _Mesembryanthemum_ name published in 1912.
Its documented wild range extends from southeastern Botswana into South Africa.

Lithops heads are paired succulent leaves with a fissure between them. Their
translucent upper windows admit light into the leaf body. During renewal, the new leaf pair often has a fissure roughly at right angles
to the old one. More than one head can form in a renewal cycle, gradually
building a small cluster.

## Care and watch points

Use the [shared planter's care record](./lithops-shared-planter.md#care-in-this-collection)
and the [short Lithops watering strategy](../../watering-quick-guide.md#lithops-check-each-head).
Inspect these two heads individually and compare their stage with the grey/green
group before making a whole-pot watering decision.

The September 23 repot record describes all four heads as firm. An aging outer
pair can shrink as replacement leaves develop; that alone is not a thirst signal.
Use actual root-zone dryness, current-leaf condition, and growth stage together.
Acclimate to stronger light gradually, and compare photographs under similar
lighting to follow changes in color and new growth.
