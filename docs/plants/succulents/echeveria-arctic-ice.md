## Identity and breeder history

The working name is _Echeveria_ 'Arctic Ice', a horticultural hybrid in the
Crassulaceae family. “Blue hen and chicks” is Altman's retail common name;
it does not identify the plant as a hardy _Sempervivum_.

The primary cultivar record is **USPP29584P2**, issued **August 7, 2018**.
It names **Renee O'Connell** as inventor and Altman Specialty Plants as the
original assignee. A February 2012 cross in Vista, California, combined seed
parent **'E1 IV'** with pollen parent **'Lila's Surprise'**; selection followed
in March 2013. These are cultivar names, not a verified species-level pedigree.
Similarity to _E. elegans_ does not establish that species as a parent.

The patent describes pale, opalescent rosettes, thick spoon-shaped leaves with
small pointed tips, and moderate offsetting. Its example plants were about
15 cm across four months after cutting; that is a recorded growing result,
not this plant's measurement or a mature-size ceiling. Flowers have fused,
bell-shaped corollas with red/orange outer surfaces and yellow tips/interiors.
Its spring–summer flowering observations are not an indoor bloom schedule.

The breeder reports improved _Fusarium echeveriae_ resistance; this is a
breeder observation, not immunity to root rot. Some comparison passages contain
inconsistent cultivar naming and are excluded here. The glaucous coating can
rub away to expose greener tissue: handle the pot and preserve that powdery
surface, commonly called farina.

## Seller claims and useful distinctions

The Altman retail page inspected **October 6, 2026,** matches the supplied
screenshot's cultivar, **USPP29,584**, and **SKU 0850821**. It describes
approximately 6-inch rosettes, bright filtered light, a draining succulent mix,
and late-winter/spring flowers. Its 3.5-inch nursery-pot description does not
confirm the Home Depot specimen's container.

The retail page's top-inch-dry watering trigger is too little evidence to apply
automatically to an unfamiliar ceramic pot. Altman's wholesale cultivar page
instead says to water thoroughly after the soil dries completely. For this
collection, check the root zone and eventual pot-weight trend before deciding.

“Pet-friendly” and “non-toxic” remain **seller claims** here; no independent
toxicology reference specific to this hybrid was verified. Neither appearance
nor resemblance to another named echeveria is a basis for a stronger statement.

## Provisional care in this collection

NC State's genus guidance supports bright light, good drainage, and frost
protection. It describes fleshy rosettes, offsets, and flower stalks raised above
the leaves; these are useful general context, not confirmation of this specimen.
Placement under the collection's lights remains open until its condition and
actual position are documented. Start with bright exposure and adjust gradually;
do not copy a neighbor's measured light value without measuring this position.

The **6-inch pot may or may not fit the roots**. RHS warns that surplus potting
mix in an oversized container can remain wet and encourage rot. Check the
inside dimensions, depth, drainage opening, and root-ball size before choosing
direct planting. If it lacks drainage, a suitably sized draining inner pot is
a practical display option; remove it for watering and empty collected water.

Use a freely draining succulent medium. Once watering is warranted, wet the root
zone and let excess water escape. Avoid leaving the pot in standing water.
No first-water date, gram threshold, mix ratio, or watering interval is assigned
before the current medium and root condition are known.

Watch for persistent wetness, soft or collapsing tissue, weak new growth, and
mealybugs or scale. RHS links overwatering with root damage and rot, while low
light can produce weak growth. Inspect sheltered leaf bases and the pot before
placing it among established plants; these are observation prompts, not a
diagnosis or a reason to apply a treatment preemptively.
