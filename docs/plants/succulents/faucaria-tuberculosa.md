## Origin and growth form

Kew accepts _Faucaria tuberculosa_ and records it as a succulent subshrub native
to South Africa's Cape Provinces, with a more specific Fort Beaufort-area native
range. The name was first published as _Mesembryanthemum tuberculosum_ before
being combined in _Faucaria_. The opposing leaves form short clustered shoots
rather than the persistent woody stems of many ordinary houseplants.

SANBI describes _F. tuberculosa_ with 4–8 thick leaves per rosette and variable numbers of blunt white tubercles. Plants can remain solitary or form clumps; the flowers are yellow.

## Rotation, propagation, and watch points

- Use a consistent orientation in comparison photographs; rotate as needed
  for even growth.
- Distinguish ordinary old-leaf drying from translucent, spreading softness at
  the crown or base. The latter deserves a root and rot inspection rather than
  more water.
- Do not pull attached leaves or teeth merely because they look dry. Remove
  material only when it is fully detached or when a documented health problem
  requires intervention.
- Offsets can gradually form a cluster; separate them only when they have an established root system and the plant is healthy.
