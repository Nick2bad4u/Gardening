The grey sides and open olive windows favor _L. salicola_. Similar windows
occur in _L. olivacea_ and some forms of _L. lesliei_. White flowers would favor
_L. salicola_, whereas these alternatives usually have yellow flowers,
sometimes with a white center.

## Botanical context

Kew accepts _Lithops salicola_ L.Bolus, published in 1936, as a succulent
perennial native to South Africa's Northern Cape and Free State. SANBI describes
its wild habitat as low limestone ridges or slopes, often beside brackish pans.

SANBI's 2018 assessment lists the wild species as Near Threatened, citing its
restricted distribution and continuing habitat loss to crop cultivation.

## Care and watch points

Use the [shared planter's care record](./lithops-shared-planter.md#care-in-this-collection)
and the [short Lithops watering strategy](../../watering-quick-guide.md#lithops-check-each-head).
Inspect the grey and tan heads individually through leaf replacement, and
reassess the shared arrangement if their watering needs diverge.

The September 23 repot record describes all heads as firm. Neither a dry-looking
surface nor an old leaf pair shrinking around replacements independently calls
for water. Check the root zone and current leaves together. Introduce stronger
light gradually. Track firmness, scars and basal condition alongside the
normal color differences between the two groups.
