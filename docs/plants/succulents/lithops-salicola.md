Keep **probable** visible wherever the species is displayed. The nursery labels
say only _Lithops_ spp. Green describes typical _L. salicola_ flowers as white,
whereas the two alternatives above generally have yellow flowers, sometimes
with a white center. A flower documented for this particular group could
therefore help reassess the working ID. No flowering observation is currently
claimed for these plants.

## Botanical context

Kew accepts _Lithops salicola_ L.Bolus, published in 1936, as a succulent
perennial native to South Africa's Northern Cape and Free State. SANBI describes
its wild habitat as low limestone ridges or slopes, often beside brackish pans.
This is background for the proposed species, not a reason to add limestone or
salt to the recorded container mix.

SANBI's 2018 assessment lists the wild species as Near Threatened, citing its
restricted distribution and continuing habitat loss. This conservation status
concerns wild populations; it neither confirms the identity nor establishes
wild origin for these Altman nursery plants.

## Care and watch points

Use the [shared planter's care record](./lithops-shared-planter.md#care-in-this-collection)
and the [short Lithops watering strategy](../../watering-quick-guide.md#lithops-check-each-head).
The two probable species share one root volume, but their visible leaf stages
still need individual inspection. Reassess the arrangement if their actual
watering needs diverge rather than assuming that shared soil synchronizes them.

The September 23 repot record describes all heads as firm. Neither a dry-looking
surface nor an old leaf pair shrinking around replacements independently calls
for water. Check the root zone and current leaves together. Introduce stronger
light gradually; the proposed species is not a reason to force a darker color
or copy another container's exposure. Keep changes to firmness, scars, and the
base distinct from normal color differences between the two groups.
