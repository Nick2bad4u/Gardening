# Ornamental cactus seed starting

A small-pot, clear-cover guide for ornamental cactus seeds, with practical notes
for the available zip bags, unidentified houseplant soil, and grow lights. Use
the [Astrophytum asterias companion](./astrophytum-asterias-seed-starting.md) for
that species and retain the seed supplier's instructions for the actual lot.

Reviewed: 2026-10-05. This is a proposed setup, not a recorded sowing.

## Start with the supplies actually available

The owner reported houseplant soil, zip bags, and the ability to moisten the
medium on October 5. The soil's product name, ingredients, fertilizer content,
and storage history are unknown. Do not assume it is the loose Carlson's mix
or Molly's mix already in the [equipment inventory](./equipment/inventory.md).

Use a **small pot with drainage inside the clear bag**. The bag provides a cover;
it is not the container for loose soil and seeds. Have a waterproof tray, a label,
and clean tools ready. [UMN's seed-starting guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/starting-seeds-indoors)
supports draining containers and a fresh, fine seed-starting medium.

**Unidentified houseplant soil is the weak point.** Check the bag before using
it. A fresh seed-sowing compost is a more predictable starting material than a
coarse or heavily fertilized houseplant blend. The RHS cactus method adds
horticultural grit or sharp sand to seed compost; this is not a reason to copy
the adult collection's Molly's/perlite percentages. If the available soil stays
muddy after draining or its contents cannot be established, obtain a suitable
seed medium before sowing. That is a practical judgment, not a tested verdict on
this unidentified product.

## Arrange the pot and clear bag

This follows the [RHS cactus seed method](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/types/cacti-succulents/houseplants/growing-guide)
with a ventilated cover. It does not assume a sterile enclosure that can remain
unopened for months.

1. Clean the pot and tools. Fill the draining pot with the chosen seed medium.
2. Moisten the medium gently and allow excess water to drain. Empty the tray;
   leave no reservoir for the pot to sit in.
3. Sow sparingly. The RHS leaves small cactus seeds uncovered and covers larger
   seeds with grit; follow any specific covering instruction for the named lot.
4. Stand the pot upright inside the bag, leaving clear space above the surface.
   Close the zip as a humidity cover. Keep the plastic away from the lamp.
5. Place it in bright, indirect light. The RHS gives about **21°C / 70°F** as
   general cactus germination guidance, not an optimum for every species.
6. Open the cover daily, remove condensation, and inspect the medium. Reclose
   after inspection; keep it moist without waterlogging. Do not water merely
   because a day has passed.

Keep the bag out of direct sunshine. Check temperature beside the pot during
the lighting period; the room's temperature does not establish the enclosure's
temperature. A hot enclosure calls for a cooler placement, not extra water.

## Light: measure lux at the seed surface

**There is no defensible universal "cactus seed lumens" setting.** These units
answer different questions:

| Measurement | What it tells you                                                                       |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Lumens      | A lamp's visible-light output; it does not specify what reaches a pot.                  |
| Lux         | Visible light reaching a surface. The sensor belongs at seed height.                    |
| PPFD        | Photosynthetic photon flux at the surface, measured with an appropriate quantum sensor. |

[Apogee's conversion reference](https://www.apogeeinstruments.com/conversion-ppfd-to-lux/)
shows that lux-to-PPFD factors vary with the light source. Keep the **raw lux**
from the documented **UNI-T UT383BT**. An app conversion remains an estimate,
especially when daylight and different LED spectra are mixed.

For this collection, use a separate gentle-light position and measure it.
The established AW200/AeroLight settings and adult-plant readings do not
establish seedling exposure. The inventory also records two Juhefa fixtures;
an available separate fixture could simplify adjustment, but no fixture or
distance has been selected for seeds.

Measure at the intended surface height, checking the center and edges with the
cover in its normal position where practical. Record lamp, distance, dimming,
raw lux, light period, and enclosure temperature. Recheck after moving anything.
Do not put a wet meter into the medium. This measurement plan is a practical
inference for comparing the actual setup, not a species light requirement.

A **12-hour daytime light period** is a reasonable first trial within UMN's
general 12–16-hour seedling guidance. It is not a demonstrated cactus optimum.
Keep a dark period, and do not change the adult display's timer just to match
this trial. No validated seed-stage lux range was established by the sources
reviewed here; the companion explains the limits of the species experiments.
Its [light study](./astrophytum-asterias-seed-starting.md#what-the-species-research-supports)
includes a reported 500 lux LED condition for context, without establishing an
optimum or a range for other cactus species.

## After emergence

Continue observing moisture and growth. The adult collection's dry-weight
plateau and fertilizer recipes are not seedling instructions. Use plain water
for germination; seeds initially supply their own energy. The eventual feeding
decision depends on the medium's existing nutrients and established growth.

Reduce cover gradually as seedlings establish, checking that the newly exposed
medium does not dry abruptly. This is a cautious adaptation, with no fixed
unbagging date. Transplant when seedlings can be handled without damaging them,
not because a preset number of weeks has elapsed.

## Problems worth acting on

| Observation                                                        | Practical response                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Standing water or persistently saturated medium                    | Drain excess and reassess the medium and watering.                                                                                            |
| Thin, collapsing, water-soaked seedlings or spreading fuzzy growth | Separate the affected pot and inspect promptly; these can fit damping-off symptoms. Avoid shared drainage water and clean tools between pots. |
| Elongated new growth                                               | Review light at seed height and lamp spectrum; do not compensate by flooding the pot.                                                         |
| Bleaching, marked discoloration, or an overheated cover            | Reduce exposure and check temperature. Color alone does not identify the cause.                                                               |

[UMN's damping-off guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/yard-and-garden-problems/how-to-prevent-seedling-damping-off)
supports clean materials, drainage, and avoiding excess moisture, fertilizer
salts, and poor growing conditions. Do not treat a sealed bag as disease control.

## Keep a small record

Record the packet's botanical name, supplier, seed count, sowing date, medium,
pot, and first emergence. Add one setup photograph and the measured light and
temperature. Note changes when they happen. A seed tray does not need a new
weighing schedule or a collection P-ID before its actual planting is documented.
