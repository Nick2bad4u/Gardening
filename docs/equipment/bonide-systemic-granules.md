# Bonide systemic-granule guide

Root-absorbed insect control for non-edible container plants. The owned
**Systemic Houseplant Insect Control, 8 oz** granules contain **0.22% imidacloprid**,
EPA **53883-217-4**.

[Back to pest-control supplies](./pest-control-supplies.md)

## Best fit

An option for listed insects feeding on **non-edible container plants**, including
houseplants. Roots absorb the insecticide and it moves into the plant; feeding
insects ingest it. This is a soil application rather than a spray for a visible
bug. Bonide describes protection of new growth for **up to eight weeks**.
[Product information][product] and [label][label]

Targets include aphids, mealybugs, whiteflies, soft scales, thrips, and fungus-gnat
larvae. **It does not control spider mites**; choose a mite-labeled option if
magnification confirms mites. [Label][label] and [Extension comparison][extension]

## Apply to the container

1. Check the pot's previous treatment record first. A new bottle does not restart
   the treatment interval.
2. Measure dry granules for the container size. These examples come directly from
   the label; use its full table for other listed sizes:

   | Container diameter | Granules       |
   | ------------------ | -------------- |
   | 4 inches           | 2½ teaspoons   |
   | 6 inches           | 2½ tablespoons |
   | 8 inches           | ⅕ cup          |

3. Distribute evenly and incorporate into the upper **1–2 inches** of medium,
   avoiding root damage. Water thoroughly to activate; avoid heavy watering for
   the next **10 days**, as directed on the label.
4. Reserve the measuring utensil for pesticide use. [Application directions and
   rate table][label]

For this collection, dose a shared planter **once as one container**, not once
per botanical profile. Do not estimate a rate from the plant count or round an
unlisted pot size upward. Record the amount, container ID, date, and watering
event together so later inspections have a useful baseline.

Lithops and split rock need a separate watering decision before any proposed
application. Granules requiring water-in are not a reason to interrupt their dry
cycle casually. The previously recorded September 26 application remains the
treatment history; this guide does not add another application.

## Recheck and repeat

Inspect the affected areas for live pests and fresh damage rather than adding
more granules when an old mark remains. The label gives an **eight-week growing
season application interval**. Use that instruction when continuing a justified
treatment; keep routine observation separate from automatic redosing.
[Label][label]
