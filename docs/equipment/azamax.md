# AzaMax insecticide guide

A concentrate for foliar treatment or pest-specific soil drenches. The owned
**General Hydroponics GH2045, 4 fl oz** bottle contains **1.2% azadirachtin**,
EPA **71908-1-81268**.

[Back to pest-control supplies](./pest-control-supplies.md)

## Best fit

A flexible option for an identified pest on indoor ornamentals. AzaMax reduces
feeding and interferes with pest development; assess changing pest activity and
new damage rather than expecting every insect to drop immediately. The
manufacturer describes foliar and root-zone applications and emphasizes reaching
leaf undersides and crevices. [Product information][product]

Label targets include aphids, mealybugs, whiteflies, scales, thrips, spider mites,
and fungus-gnat larvae. Foliar treatment and soil drenching are separate methods;
choose by where the pest lives. [Package label][label]

## Foliar use

1. Identify the affected plant and inspect nearby pots. Photograph the pest and
   mark the areas to recheck.
2. Wear chemical-resistant gloves. Test a small area first; the label notes that
   treatment can reduce waxy bloom.
3. The label's **lower foliar range is 0.32–1.0 fl oz per US gallon**. Select the
   rate for pest pressure using its foliar table; measure concentrate, never
   estimate by capfuls.
4. Mix with water, cover pest hiding places, and re-agitate after standing. Use
   prepared spray within **24 hours**. [Label][label]

For this collection, examine areoles and leaf joints as well as broad leaf
surfaces. Keep a treatment note with the dilution and plant ID so the next
inspection compares the same locations.

## Fungus-gnat drench

The soil-drench directions specify **1 tablespoon per US gallon of water** for
fungus gnats: the **0.4%** product dilution. Apply **1 pint of finished mixture per
gallon of soil**, avoiding excessive leaching. This is not the hydroponic recipe.
[Label][label]

For Lithops and split rock, first decide whether a drench fits the current dry
cycle. Their shared shelf does not make them part of another pot's watering or
treatment. Trap catches are a useful inspection prompt, not a dose calculation.

## Recheck and repeat

The label describes **7–10-day foliar intervals** and **2–3 drenches at 10–14-day
intervals**, with separate high-pressure directions. Follow the selected method
for an active pest problem; these are not a standing collection-wide calendar.
[Label][label]
