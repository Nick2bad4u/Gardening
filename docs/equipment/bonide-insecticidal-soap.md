# Bonide Insecticidal Soap

A direct-contact option for small, exposed pests on indoor, outdoor, and
greenhouse plants. This is **standard product 652**, a **32 fl oz ready-to-use**
spray with **1% potassium salts of fatty acids**, EPA **67702-13-4**.

[Back to pest-control supplies](./pest-control-supplies.md)

## Best fit

- **Aphids, mealybugs, spider mites, whiteflies, thrips, and scale insects** are
  listed targets. Thorough coverage matters more than leaving a coating behind:
  the spray must contact the pest.
- Useful when you can reach insects under leaves, along veins, or around joints.
  Soap has **no residual insect-killing activity after it dries**, so missed
  insects can remain and another inspection matters.
- This is **not Captain Jack's Super Soap**, which has a different formulation.
  It is also not a systemic treatment or a soil-drench recipe for root pests.

## How to use

1. **Try a small area first**, especially on a plant you have not treated with
   soap before. Wait at least **24 hours** and check for injury before wider use;
   longer observation is sensible if the response is unclear.
2. **Shake the bottle and spray as supplied without dilution.**
3. **Directly wet the pests and affected plant surfaces**, including leaf
   undersides. Rotate the pot and check tucked-away areas rather than merely
   misting from above.
4. **Avoid full sun.** For this shelf setup, treat away from the close grow
   lights and return the plant after it dries. Note the date and inspect both
   pest activity and the plant's response afterward.

## Recheck and limits

The label permits repeat treatment **weekly or bi-weekly if needed**. Reassess
surviving pests and newly affected growth before repeating treatment.

The label excludes **sweet peas, nasturtiums, and delicate ferns**, and calls for
care with new seedlings, transplants, and blooms. Extension guidance also notes
that some succulents, including jade, can be soap-sensitive. That makes a test
particularly worthwhile for this collection's waxy succulents, cacti, and mesembs.

For Lithops and split rock, keep this a targeted foliar decision: do not soak the
pot or change its watering cycle just to apply soap.
