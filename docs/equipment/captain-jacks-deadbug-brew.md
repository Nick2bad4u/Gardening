# Captain Jack's Deadbug Brew

An **outdoor residential** option for listed feeding insects on ornamentals and
garden crops. The owned **product 250, 32 fl oz ready-to-use** bottle contains
**spinosad 0.001%**, EPA **4-472**. These directions are for that bottle, not the
concentrate or Super Soap.

[Back to pest-control supplies](./pest-control-supplies.md)

## Best fit

- Especially useful to consider for identified **thrips, caterpillars,
  leaf-feeding beetles, or leafminers** on labeled outdoor plants.
- **Spider mites** are also listed for outdoor ornamentals.
- The ornamental pest list does **not list mealybugs, aphids, or scale**. For
  those common collection pests, compare the other guides instead of assuming
  the Deadbug name covers everything.

## How to use

1. **Apply outdoors in a sheltered spot suited to the plant.** If taking a
   houseplant outside for treatment, avoid sudden strong sun on indoor-grown
   leaves or stems.
2. **Shake well and use directly.** Do not dilute or add another product.
3. **Wet both leaf surfaces to the point of runoff**, following the directions
   for outdoor ornamentals. Keep runoff out of drains. Choose calm weather.
4. **Allow time to work.** Feeding can stop before insects die several hours
   later. An insect visible immediately afterward is not a reason to spray again.

## Recheck and limits

For **outdoor spider mites**, treat early, before webbing, and reapply after
**7–10 days** for newly hatched nymphs as needed. Edible crops have separate
intervals and limits in the label table.

Protect bees for **3 hours after treatment**: do not spray flowering,
pollen-shedding, or nectar-producing parts if bees may visit during that period.
Choose a **24-hour rain-free** window.

## Fit for this collection

Keep this as an outdoor-use option in the arsenal. For indoor treatment of the
Peperomia, oyster plant, cacti, or mesembs, first compare products whose labels
include indoor houseplants. Before wider use on an unfamiliar specimen, a small
trial area is a practical way to check its response. Keep treatment records
separate from purchase quantities; an already-used bottle alone does not tell us
which plants were treated or when.
