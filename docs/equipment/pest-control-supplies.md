## Choose by the problem

- **One unfamiliar bug or pale deposit:** inspect it with the magnifier,
  photograph it, and check nearby areoles, leaf joints, and undersides before
  choosing a spray.
- **Flying gnats:** sticky cards and Zevo show adult activity. Neither treats
  larvae in the medium; identify the catch before assuming every fly is a fungus
  gnat.
- **Exposed mealybugs, aphids, scale, or whiteflies:** soap and Garden Safe have
  relevant label-listed targets. These contact products need to reach the pest.
- **Spider mites:** soap, Garden Safe, and AzaMax have mite uses; Deadbug also
  lists mites for its outdoor ornamental use. **The systemic granules are not a
  spider-mite treatment.**
- **Persistent identified pests:** choose a product labeled for the pest, plant,
  and setting, then recheck the plant's response. Several products here overlap;
  owning them provides alternatives, not a reason to use all of them in sequence.

The individual guides summarize the linked manufacturer labels. Inspection and
selection are collection guidance, not a fixed treatment calendar.

## Monitoring, inspection, and storage

Zevo uses blue/UV light and adhesive to catch flying insects. Place it near activity with its light unobstructed, away from
competing screens and somewhere dry. Inspect its cartridge and the yellow cards
for catches, and replace spent adhesive. These are tools for flying adults;
they do not treat larvae in roots or medium. [Zevo guidance][zevo] and
[fungus-gnat monitoring][csu]

The magnifier makes it easier to examine areoles, leaf axils, and undersides;
swabs help with precise local cleaning. For cacti, mesembs, Peperomia, and oyster
plant, test an unfamiliar foliar product on a small area before wider treatment.
Keep products in their original labeled containers, secured away from children,
animals, and food, and follow each product's storage directions.
