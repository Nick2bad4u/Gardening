**Recommended first trial:** start with the **FECiDA alone for 12 daytime hours**, keeping the two Juhefas available as targeted fill if measurements show dim areas after adjusting the six heads. Leave the optional Diivoo out of that first trial. The six adjustable heads offer more positioning options for both canopies; more even coverage is an intended benefit to verify after setup. Both plants have bright-indirect nursery guidance; low-light survival is not the same as compact, well-colored growth. NC State qualifies Peperomia's low-light tolerance as especially applicable to non-variegated cultivars and warns that insufficient light can make oyster plants lanky and less purple. Give the oyster plant priority for the stronger measured position, while checking both canopies.

Use leaf-height measurements to choose a gentler starting exposure. The following are **conservative collection-specific trial ranges inferred from bright-indirect care guidance**, not published cultivar thresholds or measurements. Iowa State's broader foliage-houseplant categories are 3–6 DLI for low light and 6–10 for medium light; those categories support the comparison without establishing an exact requirement for either cultivar.

| Plant                          | Initial comparison range | Possible settled range after acclimation                |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| P31 / #7 Peperomia Bicolor     | About 4–6 mol·m⁻²·day⁻¹  | About 6–10, if new growth remains compact and undamaged |
| P32 / #8 Tricolor oyster plant | About 6–8 mol·m⁻²·day⁻¹  | About 8–12, if new growth remains compact and undamaged |

The previously reported far-table **18–22 app-estimated DLI** is above these proposed starting comparisons and is not the default new-arrival spot. It is not a measured burn threshold either. Increase exposure gradually only after observing the plants and their root-zone moisture. Keep phone/app estimates distinct from calibrated PAR measurements, and do not change the existing light settings merely to fit a newly purchased basket. Species care evidence and the limits of the inference are linked in each profile.

For the new room, a **12–14-hour daytime light window** is a reasonable initial trial, consistent with University of Minnesota foliage-houseplant guidance. The exact listings offer FECiDA 4/8/12/16/20-hour, Juhefa 4/8/12-hour, and optional Diivoo 4/8/12/18-hour timer choices, so **12 hours is a shared first setting**, with the selected lights aligned to the same daytime window. A 14-hour preset and restart behavior after interrupted power are not established. An applied timer setting has not been reported. The old cactus-room 13 h 15 m program does not establish this setup's runtime. The higher settled ranges above are optional comparisons, not a schedule for increasing light when growth is already satisfactory.

For those initial DLI ranges, the equivalent constant PPFD is approximately as follows, in **µmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹**:

| Pot      | 12-hour PPFD | 14-hour PPFD |
| -------- | ------------ | ------------ |
| P31 / #7 | 93–139       | 79–119       |
| P32 / #8 | 139–185      | 119–159      |

These PPFD equivalents assume constant light throughout the stated hours. **DLI = average PPFD × hours × 0.0036**; for example, 100 PPFD for 14 hours supplies 5.04 DLI. The intended comparison is the total from lamps plus daylight, so the lamps need not supply the entire dose where measured daylight contributes meaningfully.

1. Measure **the FECiDA alone after dark** for the recommended first trial. Check representative upper, outer, and shaded leaf positions at each plant's actual canopy height, recording distances, control settings, and runtime. The owned **UNI-T UT383BT with Android PPFD Meter** can provide useful estimates; retain raw lux and the app's spectrum setting, because conversion for the lamps' spectra is not a calibrated PAR measurement.
2. Assess daylight separately over the day, including a dim or overcast day. A noon daylight-plus-LED reading multiplied by the lamp timer overstates variable daylight. Check whether P32 still shades P31 despite the stool; a small sideways offset may help preserve a view of the glass. Repeat the comparison as winter daylight changes.
3. For initial setup, roughly three FECiDA heads over each plant is a practical starting arrangement. Spread them over the canopy and outer foliage, then favor P32's stronger comparison range as readings justify. Adjust each head to the actual leaves: the stool changes P31's height, so equal head heights need not give equal clearances or exposure. Moving lamps closer can raise local intensity while shrinking coverage. Follow the supplied clearance guidance, keep foliage clear of hot fixtures and cold glass, and leave the sliding door's travel clear.
4. Reposition the FECiDA heads first. If a part of either canopy remains underlit, add **one Juhefa aimed at that area**, then remeasure with all selected lights on together. Add the second only if another gap remains. The Juhefas and Diivoo list dimming; FECiDA dimming remains unverified. Avoid concentrating beams on one central patch. The Diivoo remains optional; compare it on/off at the same leaf positions only if considering it later. Acclimate gradually and watch for stretching, weaker color, bleaching, or scorch before considering any further purchase.

The living-room plan does not establish its temperature, humidity, airflow, or drying rate. Observe those conditions after the move and retain the existing houseplant moisture checks; moving alone does not reset a pot's weighing setup.

## Compare the Plan With a Phone Meter

The [blank reading sheet](../../assets/layouts/2026-09-15-four-tables/relative-readings.csv) is a **historical template** with 24 front coordinates, five rear-container points and a former windowsill point. For a new comparison, use the current grid above, include P35/P36/P37, and record Money Tree's stool and the terrarium desk separately. Do not treat the template's old windowsill label as the current location.

1. Use both fixtures at their usual steady settings, outside the sunrise/sunset ramps. Record which light is on which side, each spectrum mode, and the actual fixture positions. Keep blinds and daylight consistent, ideally with little daylight contribution.
2. Use the same phone, app, upward sensor orientation and any diffuser required by that app. Avoid shading it with your body. Let readings settle and repeat several positions; clipping or erratic values invalidate a ranking.
3. First compare the currently recorded table positions at **one recorded common horizontal height**, clear of obstructions where practical. Do not mix this pass with readings at different tip heights. Repeat **R2C3** before, during and after the pass to check drift.
4. Make a separate pass at actual growing-surface heights for D3, F2, A1, B3, C2, H1, H2, G3 and the recorded rear pots, including P35/P36. For #1 compare the pale column, green column and trailing stems separately; for #2 compare low rosette and taller foliage. Record the height with each reading. Separately measure #3 at its actual leaves above the room-side stool; record support height and daylight/blinds. Record the terrarium's leaf-level exposure separately from external-glass readings. Do not fold this different-height, mixed-daylight reading into the common-height table ranking.
5. Compare corners, interior and rear shadows. If values repeat within a small range, preserve spacing instead of chasing small differences. The earlier completed comparison led only to the confirmed A3/F1 exchange. The October 22 photographs document later placement differences without new settings or light measurements; a future comparison should answer a specific remaining question rather than trigger another automatic reshuffle.

Lux measures illuminance, not lumens. Lux-to-PPFD conversion depends on spectrum; with two potentially different spectra, even a repeatable lux ratio is only an approximate relative comparison of plant-useful light. Do not invent a conversion factor or treat uniformity as proof that absolute intensity is suitable. See [Apogee's spectrum-dependent examples](https://www.apogeeinstruments.com/conversion-ppfd-to-lux/) and [University of Minnesota Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/lighting-for-indoor-plants).
