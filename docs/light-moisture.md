# Light and moisture reference

Use each plant's **Light & moisture** card for a working light comparison and a
physical watering check. These are collection trials and observation triggers,
not measured conditions, fixed watering dates, or proven species limits.

The website's comparison list uses the same maintained records as the plant
cards. Shared-pot profiles remain separate botanical references with one pot
history and one watering decision.

## Minimum, good, optimal, and maximum

- **Below the working range:** inspect placement and new growth. This is not a
  proven survival minimum; a shaded or recovering plant may need gradual changes.
- **Working range:** a practical trial for an established plant, adjusted through
  observations. Some profiles also show a gentler starting comparison.
- **Optimal:** not established for these individual plants, pots, and conditions.
  A productive range is not proof of a precise optimum.
- **Above the working range:** inspect heat, bleaching, scorch, and water stress.
  The upper edge is not a guaranteed safe maximum or a damage threshold.

Numeric entries are labeled **collection trial ranges**. Sources often establish
qualitative light preferences or broad plant categories; the card's source notes
state where the numeric choice is an inference. Uncertain mixed plantings retain
qualitative guidance. The removed plant's card stays historical.

## Reading PPFD, DLI, and lux

**PPFD** measures photosynthetic photons reaching a leaf area each second, in
µmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹. **DLI** adds that exposure over a day, in mol·m⁻²·day⁻¹.
The cards convert their DLI ranges into **12 hours of constant light** so plants
can be compared on the same basis. This does not change a timer or describe an
existing installation.

For constant light, **DLI = PPFD × hours × 0.0036**. For example, 100 PPFD for
12 hours is 4.32 DLI; the same intensity for 16 hours is 5.76 DLI. A ramped lamp or
changing window light needs readings through the day rather than multiplying one
peak reading by the whole day. The maintained cactus schedule remains in the
[current light setup](./setup.md).

**Lux** describes light weighted for human vision. The cards show an approximate
white-LED comparison envelope using **55–70 lux per 1 PPFD**. This rounds the
conversion factors for selected 2700–5000 K white LEDs in Lighting Analysts'
spectral table. It is not a calibration of our installed lights, and the envelope
is not a statistical confidence interval. It combines the low DLI edge with the
lower factor and the high edge with the upper factor.

Do not apply that estimate to colored lamps, red-enhanced grow lights, or mixed
daylight and LED exposure without a suitable spectrum-specific conversion. A
fixture's exact spectral data or a quantum sensor is preferable. PPFD displays
are rounded to about 5 units and lux to about 100; those rounded numbers do not
imply measurement precision.

## Measure where the plant receives light

1. Measure at leaf height, with the sensor oriented toward the light as the leaf
   would be. Keep your hand and body from shading it.
2. Record the light source, dimmer setting, distance, sensor, time, and duration.
   Record glass or other barriers and which side of them held the sensor.
3. Check the brightest and dimmest leaves. One central reading does not describe
   a whole plant, shelf, or mixed pot.
4. Increase exposure gradually and compare new growth and stress signs. A newly
   purchased, recently repotted, variegated, or recovering plant needs judgment
   before moving toward the upper end of a trial range.

The terrarium's owner-reported lux readings remain dated observations; they do
not establish a target for all of its provisionally identified members.

## Moisture means a drying stage

The cards show **check**, **water when**, and **wait when** cues. They do not assign
universal moisture percentages or a meter scale: sensor readings depend on the
probe, substrate, contact, and calibration. “Moist” is a physical observation,
not a fixed percentage. Air humidity and a dry-looking surface do not establish
root-zone moisture.

Use the actual pot, drainage, medium, accessible root zone, and plant condition.
Weight trends can support that inspection when the pot has a useful baseline;
a low weight or drying alert alone does not prove that watering is appropriate.

- Most cacti need a dry root zone before a growing-season watering decision.
- Many leafy succulents dry between drinks, with seasonal and plant-condition
  exceptions.
- Lithops and split rocks need a leaf-cycle check; dry mix alone is insufficient.
- Peperomias, money tree, oyster plant, and Aglaonema use partial-drying checks.
- Fittonia needs a lightly moist, aerated root zone rather than cactus-style
  whole-pot drought.
- A terrarium needs inspection for trapped water and individual plant condition.

Follow the profile's exceptions and the
[pot-by-pot watering guide](./watering-quick-guide.md). For a shared container,
inspect every member before watering the whole pot. A component profile is not
an instruction to water that component independently.

## Sources and evidence

- [Apogee Instruments: instantaneous PPFD to integrated PPFD](https://www.apogeeinstruments.com/conversion-instantaneous-ppfd-to-integrated-ppfd/)
  supplies the constant-light DLI calculation and explains changing daylight.
- [Lighting Analysts: PPFD concepts and spectral conversion factors](https://docs.agi32.com/AGi32/Content/adding_calculation_points/PPFD_Concepts.htm)
  provides the selected white-LED examples behind the approximate lux envelope.
- [Apogee Instruments: PPFD to lux](https://www.apogeeinstruments.com/conversion-ppfd-to-lux/)
  demonstrates source-dependent factors; its CMH values are not white-LED
  calibrations.
- [Iowa State Extension: supplemental-light considerations](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/growing-indoor-plants-under-supplemental-lights/important-considerations-providing-supplemental-light-indoor-plants)
  gives broad foliage DLI categories and explains intensity, duration, and
  spectrum. Those categories do not prove cultivar-specific limits.
- Each plant card includes its horticultural sources and the scope of their
  support. Existing collection trial ranges also remain documented in the
  [earlier fixture research](./equipment/vivosun-aw200se.md#working-light-targets-for-this-collection).
