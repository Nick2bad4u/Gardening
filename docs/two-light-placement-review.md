This review covers the **30 recorded pots: 24 on wood and six on glass**, including the living components of the shared containers. It uses the collection records and installed-light information confirmed through September 14, 2026. The [current placement guide](./layouts/table-placement-research.md) publishes the adopted F2/E3 refinement and the audit of its effects on neighboring plants, space, fixture assumptions, and care records. ## What Changes About the Earlier Advice

The earlier advice to keep the layout was too broad. It correctly avoided an unnecessary complete reshuffle, but it did not give enough weight to the useful open-left edge or distinguish the individual plants within the large “Bright filtered light” category. It also gave Raindrops too much priority for the strongest exposure simply because it is an Echeveria.

Three corrections matter. First, a plant's light requirement and the intensity at its current slot are different questions. Second, an outdoor “full sun” or “partial shade” description is useful horticultural evidence, but it is not a calibrated intensity target for 13 hours under these LEDs. Third, current plant size, growing-tip height, neighboring foliage, and cultivar identity can matter more than a simple cactus-versus-succulent distinction.[^01]

The strongest case is for **bright, gentler positions for Ming Thing and the money tree**, with a prudent gentler position for the small Boobie. Feather Cactus, Serpent Cactus, Domino, Monk's Hood, and Raindrops also warrant protection from an excessive local peak. This does not make all of them low-light plants or mean they all need the outermost column.[^02][^03][^04][^05][^06][^07][^08]

## Why the Choice of Edge Matters

The **left, open-room wooden edge** is the most plausible place to obtain gentler exposure while keeping a plant under useful lamp light. It is farther from the likely interior of each fixture's long dimension and has no end reflector returning light from that side. That is a geometric inference, not a measured ranking: spill from both fixtures can still make the edge bright.

The **top and bottom edges are different**. The Mylar divider and white wall can return light into those sides, so row 1 or row 6 is not automatically a protected location. The wood-to-glass boundary is also not necessarily gentle: the fixtures deliberately extend across it. Moving a sensitive plant “to the perimeter” without specifying which perimeter can therefore miss the point.

There is no sharp light cutoff at the metal fixture edge. Light from an extended panel reaches plants both beneath and beyond its outline, and light from the neighboring panel can contribute too. The sensible placement rule is to use the relatively gentler lit positions for plants that benefit from them, while keeping stronger-light plants in clear exposure; the drawing alone cannot identify the brightest pot.

Height complicates the map. A tall column in the shared cactus pot and a low Echeveria on the same table receive light at different distances and can shade one another. The 18-inch reference does not establish an 18-inch clearance above every plant. The weak north window remains supplementary, so moving a succulent to outer glass should not leave it relying on that window alone.[^01]

## A Small Check That Resolves the Remaining Uncertainty

A one-time comparison is more useful than another complete layout designed from fixture rectangles. If a suitable light meter is available, compare the **F2 and E3 growing-surface positions first**, then A1 and C2 if considering that swap. Also compare Ming Thing, the tallest pale cactus tip in #1, the low H1 rosette or #6 dish, and the outer money-tree/Kiwi area. Keep the sensor orientation consistent, avoid shading it, and compare at the plants' real heights with both fixtures running at their normal steady settings.

Those comparisons answer specific questions: whether the chosen edge is gentler, whether the tall plant receives more light than the low pots, and whether the outer glass remains useful. A plant-light meter can quantify exposure; a repeatable lux reading from the same device is only a rough relative comparison, especially where different spectra contribute. A camera photograph or dimmer percentage cannot establish those values.[^01]

If no meter is available, the adopted F2/E3 swap remains a reasonable modest choice, but the inferred difference may be small. Use changes in new growth over time, persistent stretching, or new bleaching/scorched patches as reasons to reassess; do not infer damage solely from normal variegation or a colorful cultivar. The available records do not establish that any plant is currently being damaged. Existing marks cannot be expected to disappear merely because a pot has moved.

Retain **AW200 45% and AeroLight 240 W 38%** while assessing the placement change; no new evidence here supports raising or lowering the whole pair. Different fixture heights can eventually help with the tall shared cactus planter, but any height change also affects neighboring plants beneath that fixture. Neither a replacement table nor the removed room-end reflector is needed to make the selective improvements in this review.
