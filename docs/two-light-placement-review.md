# Two-Light Placement Review

The September 14 relative-light revision proposes gentler wooden corners for D3 Ming Thing and F2 Boobie, clearer interior positions for stronger-light priorities, and an inward shoulder for Kiwi aeonium. It supersedes the earlier F2/E3-only refinement and optional A1/C2 swap. These are recommended destinations, not a record that the owner has already moved the pots or measured their light.

This review covers the **30 recorded pots: 24 on wood and six on glass**, including the living components of the shared containers. It uses the collection records and installed-light information confirmed through September 14, 2026. The [current placement guide](./layouts/table-placement-research.md) publishes the proposed relative-light revision and the audit of its effects on neighboring plants, space, fixture assumptions, and care records. It does not assert a new physical measurement or plant-health observation.

## What Changes About the Earlier Advice

The earlier advice to keep the layout was too broad. It correctly avoided an unnecessary complete reshuffle, but it did not give enough weight to the useful open-left edge or distinguish the individual plants within the large “Bright filtered light” category. It also gave Raindrops too much priority for the strongest exposure simply because it is an Echeveria.

Three corrections matter. First, a plant's light requirement and the intensity at its current slot are different questions. Second, an outdoor “full sun” or “partial shade” description is useful horticultural evidence, but it is not a calibrated intensity target for 13 hours under these LEDs. Third, current plant size, growing-tip height, neighboring foliage, and cultivar identity can matter more than a simple cactus-versus-succulent distinction.[^01]

The strongest case is for **bright, gentler positions for Ming Thing and the money tree**, with a prudent gentler position for the small Boobie. Feather Cactus, Serpent Cactus, Domino, Monk's Hood, and Raindrops also warrant protection from an excessive local peak. This does not make all of them low-light plants or mean they all need the outermost column.[^02][^03][^04][^05][^06][^07][^08]

## Why the Choice of Edge Matters

The owner clarified that the fixtures cover almost the entire wooden area, with roughly an inch left at the top and bottom ends in the marked-up view and only a **½–1-inch gap between fixtures**. Treat those as approximate owner observations, not dimensions from a measured plan. At the reported height, substantial spill across this narrow gap is expected; a dark seam is unlikely from the gap alone. The AW200 wings are assumed to remain flat. The upper/lower model assignment and current spectrum modes remain unconfirmed.

The **two left, open-room wooden corners** are plausible gentler wooden positions while remaining under useful lamp light. The middle of that same edge may receive more contribution from both fixtures and should not be ranked equally dim. It is farther from the likely interior of each fixture's long dimension and has no end reflector returning light from that side. That is a geometric inference, not a measured ranking: spill from both fixtures can still make the edge bright.

The **top and bottom edges are different**. The Mylar divider and white wall can return light into those sides, so row 1 or row 6 is not automatically a protected location. The wood-to-glass boundary is also not necessarily gentle: the fixtures deliberately extend across it. Moving a sensitive plant “to the perimeter” without specifying which perimeter can therefore miss the point.

There is no sharp light cutoff at the metal fixture edge. Light from an extended panel reaches plants both beneath and beyond its outline, and light from the neighboring panel can contribute too. The sensible placement rule is to use the relatively gentler lit positions for plants that benefit from them, while keeping stronger-light plants in clear exposure; the drawing alone cannot identify the brightest pot.

Height complicates the map. A tall column in the shared cactus pot and a low Echeveria on the same table receive light at different distances and can shade one another. The 18-inch reference does not establish an 18-inch clearance above every plant. The weak north window remains supplementary, so moving a succulent to outer glass should not leave it relying on that window alone.[^01]

- **Fit:** the wood remains four columns by six rows. Most recorded pots are 4-inch class; F2 is 3-inch. Moving 4-inch E2 into row 5 in place of 3-inch F2 tightens that row relative to the preceding plan. Four 4-inch rims already span the nominal 16-inch width. - **Low-plant shadows:** H1's new neighbors include E1 above, B3 below, and G2 beside it. Give the rosette a clear light path; do not insist on the exact center of a cell if foliage blocks it. H2, G3, #5, #6, and the Echeveria in #2 need the same consideration.
- **Glass fit:** shift Kiwi only as far inward as its base and canopy allow without crowding #1 or shading #6. Do not push the low dish or square box to the weak outer rim to make the picture fit. Current dimensions of the large shared pots remain unmeasured.
- **Fixtures:** retain their horizontal axes, current dimmers, leveled suspension, and reported clearance for the first comparison. A broad mismatch may justify recentering the affected fixture after measurement; raising both dimmers to compensate for one outer pot is not the first step.
- **Care records:** a placement move is not a repot, new pot setup, watering event, or automatic daily-weighing trigger. Preserve existing wet/dry references and learn any changed drying behavior from actual observations.

## Verify the Estimated Pattern

The [placement guide](./layouts/table-placement-research.md) includes the new estimated light map, exact proposed grid, and a one-time phone-meter protocol. First compare positions at one common height, then compare the actual growing surfaces of the sensitive, low, and tall plants. Recheck a reference point during the pass and record spectrum modes. Lux is a rough relative aid, not a PPFD measurement; cross-fixture comparisons may be affected by spectrum.

The owner reports nearly full wood coverage and only a ½–1-inch gap. Differences across the wood may therefore be modest. A smoother field favors keeping good spacing rather than chasing tiny readings. The strongest unresolved contrast is likely between the near and far sides of the glass table.

Retain **AW200 45% and AeroLight 240 W 38%**, the reported height, and both 13 h 15 m programs for the comparison. No evidence currently supports changing both dimmers, restoring the room-end reflector, or replacing the table. A later fixture adjustment should address a measured distribution problem while preserving neighboring clearance.

The map is a testable hypothesis. It does not establish existing light damage, a required watering event, or a new pot setup.
