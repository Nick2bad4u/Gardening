# Two-Light Placement Review

**Historical review · September 14–20, 2026.** This records the arrangement and
lighting evidence for the 30 containers present at that time. It predates later
additions; see the [current placement guide](./layouts/table-placement-research.md)
for the complete collection.

Two **16 × 13-inch wooden tables** were added on **September 15**. The
photographs show **24 small pots in six columns × four rows** at the room end,
with six larger or shared containers behind them toward the window. Money tree
**#3 / P21** moved from the rear-left table to the north-facing windowsill by
**September 16**, leaving **29 containers on wood and one on the sill**.
Its exact move time was not recorded; #4 Kiwi remained on the rear-left table.

The four-table block was estimated from photographs at **26 inches across ×
32 inches toward the window**, excluding gaps and rails. The earlier pair was
18 inches high; the added tables' height was not measured. The former round
glass display table was no longer in use by September 20.

Orientation in this review is **window at top, open room at bottom, Mylar at
left, white wall at right**. The north-facing window supplied weak additional
daylight, and the two-foot room-end reflector was absent.

**Settings recorded for this review:** AW200 **45%**, AeroLight 240 W **38%**,
**Veg mode on both**, and **13 h 15 m total**, including **15 m sunrise and
15 m sunset**. The fixtures used four independent ceiling hooks and separate
hangers. An earlier **18-inch tip clearance** had not been remeasured after
the table rearrangement, so fixture-to-plant distance remained an open question.

## Money Tree: September Windowsill Move

**#3 / P21**, working identification _Pachira_ cf. _glabra_, was on the north-facing windowsill by September 16 in its recorded white round 6-inch pot. Its exact sill position, support dimensions, and clearance were not measured. #4 Kiwi remained rear-left, with the vacated table space open.

The goal is useful indirect light with less direct fixture exposure. Assess daylight and any LED spill at the actual leaves on the windowsill. Keep foliage clear of cold glass and drafts, and keep the pot and drainage saucer fully supported.

Downward-pointing leaves are the owner's September 15 observation. Excess light is a possibility, not a confirmed cause. [NC State's money-tree guidance](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/pachira-aquatica/) supports indirect light but describes _P. aquatica_, so it is a comparison rather than a new identification. [University of Maryland Extension](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/watering-indoor-plants) notes that both too little and too much water can cause wilting; leaf angle alone cannot distinguish these causes or establish that watering is due. Watering still depends on the pot's actual moisture and the plant's condition.

## Comparing Exposure

The [phone-comparison method](./layouts/table-placement-research.md#compare-the-plan-with-a-phone-meter) uses four rows of six small-pot readings, five rear points, and a separate north-windowsill point for #3. The supplied 48 readings were taken at individual plant heights. A future common-height comparison would be a separate dataset; do not reinterpret the existing values as that plane. Record #3 at its actual windowsill leaf height with daylight conditions; do not combine it with the common-height table ranking. Check B3 at the front, H1 at its low rosette, and local shadows from #1/#2. The dimmer settings above were the comparison baseline; these measurements alone do not establish a need to raise output.[^01][^11]

Plant response and root-zone moisture remain the basis for care after a move. A furniture rearrangement alone is not a reason to water or weigh every pot daily.
