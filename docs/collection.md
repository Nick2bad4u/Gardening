# Plant collection

Last updated: 2026-07-24

This is the current working inventory plus historical records for plants
removed from the collection. A label is useful evidence, but not a guarantee;
photo-only identifications are deliberately marked as probable.

Each record now has a [deep plant profile](./plants/) and a six-image
[licensed reference gallery](../assets/plants/). Reference photos document the
working taxon; they do not increase the confidence of a photo-only collection
ID.

## Confidence guide

- **Labeled** — came with a name and the plant is consistent with it.
- **High** — the photos, growth form, and any flower evidence make the ID a
  strong match.
- **Probable** — the best current photo match, but a flower or closer diagnostic
  photo could change it.

## Mixed succulent planter

These four plants share the rectangular planter. The species-level IDs are
strong working matches, but cultivar names should stay provisional without
labels or flowers.

| ID | Working identification | Common name | Confidence | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Succulent-01 | [*Echeveria pulidonis*](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A87059-2), or a close hybrid | — | Probable | Blue-gray rosette with fine red margins. A flower would help separate the species from lookalike hybrids. |
| Succulent-02 | [*Portulacaria afra*](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A699417-1) | Elephant bush | High | The yellow-green new growth may be a golden form such as ‘Aurea’, but the cultivar is not confirmed. |
| Succulent-03 | [*Kalanchoe bracteata*](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/kalanchoe-bracteata/common-name/silver-teaspoons/) | Silver teaspoons | Probable | The powdery silver spoon-shaped leaves fit. *Kalanchoe hildebrandtii* is a close lookalike; flowers would be useful evidence. |
| Succulent-04 | [*Kalanchoe orgyalis*](https://www.chicagobotanic.org/plant-information/plant-finder/kalanchoe-orgyalis-copper-spoons) | Copper spoons | High | This is the “brown spoons” plant. The copper felt on the leaves is normal. |

The planter can stay together while it is behaving well. The elephant bush and
kalanchoes will become woodier and taller than the echeveria, so pruning,
propagating, or separating them may eventually be useful if they crowd or shade
the rosette.

The thin green whip visible in an older photo belongs to the monkey tail. It is
etiolated growth from the low-light period, not a fifth cactus. Some of that
stretched growth has since been trimmed. The improved new growth after a month
outside is a good sign that its current light exposure is working.

Rehab-04 is no longer part of the planter or the active lighting and watering
plan. Its inventory ID is intentionally not being reused.

## Identification follow-ups

The most useful future evidence would be:

- flowers from Succulent-01 and Succulent-03;
- a clear close-up of the ribs, areoles, and base of Rehab-01;
- original nursery tags or receipts for the probable IDs.
